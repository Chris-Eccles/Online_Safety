Data protection

Data Protection Impact Assessment

Written for a school's Data Protection Officer, DSL or governors who need to see the risks we have identified and what we do about them. It sits alongside our privacy policy, GDPR page and security page.

⚠️
Draft, pending legal review. This is our own assessment, written by the person who built the service. It has not yet been reviewed by a solicitor or data protection specialist. It is an accurate technical description, not legal advice. Your school remains responsible for its own DPIA, and you are welcome to use this as an input to it.

Version 1.0 · October 2026 · reviewed at least once a year, and whenever the service changes in a way that affects personal data

1. Why we did this

Online Ready is used by children, and a school is the controller of that data. Even where a DPIA is not strictly required, we think a service for children should be able to show its working. This document covers the student course, the teacher and trust dashboards, and the ordering process.

2. What the service does

A school orders seats. Each class gets a class code. Students use the code to join, choose a display name and a 4-digit PIN, and complete seven short modules with quizzes. On passing a module they earn a certificate. Teachers see progress on a dashboard. Multi-academy trusts can see and allocate seats across their schools. Students can write short reflections in some activities.

Roles. The school is the controller for student data and we are the processor. For order and invoicing details (teacher name, email, finance contact), we are the controller.

What we do not collect from students: email address, date of birth, home address, photo, location, device identifiers for tracking, or any advertising data. There is no public sign-up, no analytics cookie, and no profiling.

Special category data. We do not ask for any. A student could choose to write something sensitive in a reflection, which is why reflections are encrypted so that we cannot read them, and why the course tells students to talk to a trusted adult rather than write about serious concerns.

4. Necessity and proportionality

Lawful basis: the school's public task or legitimate interest in educating and safeguarding pupils (Keeping children safe in education expects schools to teach online safety). For our own order data, performance of a contract. We collect the minimum needed: a student needs a name for the certificate and a PIN to resume. Every student record is deleted automatically on 1 September, whether or not the school has paid again. Students and parents can ask their school, or us, to access or delete a record at any time.

7. Children's code

Students can only join through a class code a school has issued, and we process their data only to deliver the course on the school's instruction, so the ICO's Children's code does not formally apply to us. We follow its spirit anyway: minimum data, no profiling, no nudging, no advertising, and plain language for the age group.

8. Outcome

We assess the overall residual risk to students as low, because the data is minimal, short-lived and partly encrypted, and a school controls who joins. The two items we are working to improve are the public photo storage and independent legal and security review. We do not believe the processing needs prior consultation with the ICO.

9. Actions we have committed to

  • Move safeguarding team photos to time-limited links.
  • Complete our ICO registration and have these documents reviewed by a solicitor.
  • Commission an independent security test and accessibility audit.
  • Review this DPIA every year, and whenever we change what data is collected or who processes it.
