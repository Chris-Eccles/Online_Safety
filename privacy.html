Privacy notice

Last updated 27 November 2026

In short: abity.co.uk keeps almost nothing about anyone. There are no accounts, adverts, tracking or analytics. On the main site your name stays on your own device. When you hand in a homework, your name goes only into an email to your teacher and is not kept by the website.

Who runs this site

Abity (abity.co.uk) is run by Chris Eccles, Computer Science teacher at Maidstone Grammar School, for teaching Computer Science at the school. At present it holds the Key Stage 3 (Years 7 to 9) projects. Staff CPD (cpd.abity.co.uk) is covered by the section near the end.

Badges and class questions (Years 10 to 13)

In lessons your teacher may ask a class question that you answer with the buttons at the top left of the page. Your teacher sees how many people picked each answer, never who picked what. You can also earn badges. Both are linked only to your device's random sign-in code and your group, never to your name, and are deleted with that code (and always on 1 August).

Homework (Years 10 to 13)

Homework is opened from a link your teacher shares in Teams. When you hand it in, these are sent once to the marking service and then emailed to your teacher's school mailbox (an address ending @mgs.kent.sch.uk), with two files attached (a spreadsheet and a data file):

  • your full name and group (for example 10A);
  • your answers, and the marks and feedback for each one;
  • if you were set a follow-up task, the task, your answer and its mark;
  • when you started and handed in, how long you spent on each question, how many times you pasted into each answer, and how many times you left the page.

Follow-up tasks. After you hand in, you may be given one short task based on your feedback. The homework goes to your teacher when you send that task. Until then, the website holds your marked answers for up to 7 days without your name, linked to a random code kept on your device. If you don't finish the task within 7 days, they are deleted.

The website does not keep your name or answers after the email is sent. It keeps only a scrambled code made from your name, group and the homework, which cannot be turned back into your name. It is used only to stop the same homework being handed in twice, and it is deleted every 1 August. Your answers are saved in your browser while you work, so you can come back to them.

Written answers are marked by an AI (Cloudflare Workers AI), which receives only the question, the mark scheme and your answer, never your name. The AI's marks are suggestions: your teacher's mark is the one that counts. Multiple-choice, true/false and short "quick" answers are marked automatically without AI.

What the website's database holds

  • The learning groups (for example 7Q) and each group's password, set by the teacher.
  • Which lessons the teacher has switched on for each group, and the keys that open those lessons in the page.
  • The random sign-in codes described above, linked to a group only.
  • Quiz answers (the option picked), linked to a sign-in code. Your teacher sees only totals for a group or year.
  • The homework questions and mark schemes, and the scrambled hand-in codes described above.
  • After a wrong password, a scrambled (salted and hashed) form of the device's internet address, kept for 15 minutes to block guessing. It cannot be turned back into the address.

None of this identifies a student.

Security

  • HTTPS everywhere.
  • Two passwords to get in: one for the site and one for the learning group. Admin has its own password.
  • Ten wrong passwords from one device in 15 minutes blocks that device for 15 minutes.
  • Lessons that are not switched on stay encrypted in the page.
  • Because the website holds nothing that names a student, a break-in would expose no student's personal information.

Lawful basis

The lawful basis is public task under UK GDPR Article 6(1)(e), in line with the school's other teaching records. No special category data is collected. The site is built on privacy by design: it collects the least it can and keeps it for the shortest time.

Staff CPD (cpd.abity.co.uk)

Nothing typed on Staff CPD is sent to the site. Course notes stay in the browser. The site stores the courses and resource files that are uploaded, in Supabase (London), and may show the name of the person who made a resource.

Your rights

You can ask to see, correct or delete information about you, and you can object to its use. The website holds nothing it can link to a named person, so most requests will be answered by your own device: "Sign out" clears it. To use your rights or raise a concern, speak to your Computer Science teacher or contact the school's data protection officer through the school office. If you are still unhappy, you can complain to the Information Commissioner's Office at ico.org.uk.

Our promises

  • Names are never stored by the website. A homework name goes only to your teacher's school email.
  • Results emails go only to school addresses ending @mgs.kent.sch.uk.
  • AI suggests marks for written answers but never sees names, and your teacher has the final say.
  • Sign-in codes are deleted every August.
  • No analytics, advertising or tracking.
  • No student data is used to train AI.
  • This notice will be updated whenever the site changes what it does.
